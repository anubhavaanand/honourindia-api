01 Put a billboard anywhere

Paste this anywhere in your HTML. The widget renders inside a shadow root, so it can never clash with your styles — and it only weighs a few kilobytes.

your-page.html 
<div data-honourindia></div>
<script src="/embed.js" async></script>

02 The API

Everything the widget consumes is public. Build your own displays, apps, classroom screensavers — whatever honours these minds best.

GET /api/v1/billboards/random?count=8

Random billboards: name, years, field, one-line achievement, portrait with license, and a link to read more. count between 1 and 24.

$ curl /api/v1/billboards/random?count=1
GET /api/v1/figures

The full catalogue of honoured figures, with summaries on detail pages.

$ curl /api/v1/figures
GET /api/v1/figures/:slug

One figure in full — tagline, summary, quote and links.

$ curl /api/v1/figures/anna-mani
GET /api/v1/meta

Dataset size and licensing metadata.

$ curl /api/v1/meta

Open CORS everywhere · cache-friendly headers · light rate limiting (120 req/min) · JSON only.

04 Why this exists

In India and across the developing world, the people who cured diseases, fed nations, launched rockets and decoded the universe are rarely given a billboard. Their faces don't sell phones. Their names get a paragraph in a textbook.

The ad industry built a global machine to put products in front of eyes. This project borrows that machine — the slot, the rotation, the craft — and points it at people worth looking up to. Every site that embeds a billboard donates a little corner of the internet to someone who earned it.
